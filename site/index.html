Is this for you?

A different way
to work. Worth learning.

If you build things independently and want to work with AI while deepening your own understanding, Vikix is made with you in mind.

You can build great things on Windows, macOS or another Linux desktop. The reason to try Vikix is the way it brings the work together: AI workers, project continuity, a desktop you can inspect and tools you can change.

It is a small, actively developed project. Expect a learning curve, a keyboard-focused workflow and some time in the terminal. The guides help you begin; curiosity will take you further.

A few practical questions

Before you begin.

Is Vikix free?

Yes. Vikix is free and open source under the MIT licence. You choose your AI setup: a paid provider, models on your own machine, or a compatible private endpoint. Provider fees, hardware and hosting costs depend on that choice.

Do I need to be a Linux expert?

You need a willingness to learn. The installation involves setting up Linux, and the workflow uses the keyboard and terminal. Menus and guides cover everyday tasks, and you can explore the code when you are ready.

Why not just use an AI coding tool on my current computer?

You can. Vikix adds a shared place for workers, tasks, questions, checks and handoffs, plus a desktop that can explain its own behaviour. Its value is in making those pieces work together throughout your day.

How does Omarchy fit into the story?

Omarchy inspired the idea of a complete, opinionated development desktop. Vikix follows its own direction: coordinating independent work, helping you inspect and understand the system, and making the workspace yours. Read about its foundations.

Does AI run everything automatically?

You choose when to use AI and how much freedom to give it. The Office shows progress and questions, and keeps review attached to the work. Read the agents guide for the permissions and limits of each setup.
